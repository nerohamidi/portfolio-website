    By

    Two frequencies cut the track in three. The middle band is then split once more, into what both channels hold in common — (L+R)/2, which is whatever was panned up the middle — and what they differ by, (L−R)/2, which is whatever was spread wide.

    1. held or hit A held note draws a line across the spectrogram and a hit draws one down it, so a median along time keeps one and a median along frequency keeps the other.
    2. repeats or not The loop is found from how much the track sounds like itself a few seconds later. Take the median across every turn of it and the band survives; the singer, doing something different each time, does not.
    3. centred or not Sweep a gain and watch the two channels cancel. Where they go quiet is where that sound is panned — a voice cancels in the middle, a guitar at nine o’clock does not.

    Each answer is a weight rather than a yes or no, and the four weights add to one at every point. That is what makes the four stems add back to the recording.

    How far off centre a sound may be panned and still count as a voice. Narrower turns away more of the band, and some of the voice with it.

    How hard to pull the voice out of what the three tests agreed on. More of it gets the whole voice and some of the band with it; less is cleaner and thinner.
